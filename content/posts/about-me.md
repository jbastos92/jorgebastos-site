I’m Jorge Bastos, a full stack developer from Brazil with about ten years of experience.

## Six years at Softplan

I joined Softplan in October 2020 as a junior developer. In my first three years, I worked on Sienge and Obras.gov, supporting clients such as the infrastructure departments (SEINFRA) of five Brazilian states. After my first year, I was promoted to mid-level developer.

I became the owner of the frontend projects very early. Part of the reason was my frontend background from previous jobs. The other part was timing: two developers who had been on the team for two and three years left for a company in Portugal, and someone had to take over their code.

## The "Diabo de Obras"

One of those projects was a repository called Diário de Obras (a construction site diary). Somewhere in its documentation, a past developer with a good sense of humor had nicknamed it Diabo de Obras, roughly “the construction devil.” It was an affectionate nickname, and every developer who opened the code got the joke right away: a React app from an earlier era, with class components and not a single hook, no TypeScript, render props, and a Redux store holding a bit of everything. It even had a page-turning PDF viewer that made the diary feel like a real book.

Six months later, a new team had arrived, with new developers to help. By then, though, I was already the proud, slightly tired “father” of the Diabo de Obras.

I worked on it for about eight months, alongside other tasks, and improved it a little each time I touched it: I updated the documentation, fixed bugs, and rewrote class components as function components. It wasn’t a rewrite, but I left it in better shape than I found it. It taught me how to make steady progress on legacy code without stopping delivery.

## From React to Vue

In 2023, I joined another Softplan team to work on CPA, a module of SAJ, Softplan’s software for the justice system, built for the company’s largest client, TJSP (the São Paulo State Court of Justice). There I worked as a full stack developer, and the frontend was written in Vue.js. I really enjoyed learning Vue and working across the whole stack. The team was small, just three developers: a junior frontend developer, a senior developer who specialized in Java backend, and me, so I covered a lot of ground on both sides.

My main contribution was to the notification module. It already sent web and email notifications, and the client asked for one more channel: Microsoft Teams, the main communication tool at TJSP. I prepared both the frontend and the backend for it and integrated with the Microsoft Teams API, so users could receive notifications where they already worked.

## e.CETESB

In January 2024, I went through an internal interview and moved to the e.CETESB team, where I stayed until I left Softplan in September 2026. [e.CETESB](https://e.cetesb.sp.gov.br/) is the online services platform of CETESB, São Paulo’s state environmental agency. It was an ambitious project at a challenging moment. Compared with the Diabo de Obras, the codebase was a pleasant surprise: a clear hexagonal architecture, with SOLID, DRY, and KISS as clearly defined team principles.

I helped build modules such as Reclamações (complaints), Infrações (violations), and Inspeções (inspections), some of them end to end, frontend and backend. In my last year, I took on senior-level responsibilities. From January 2026, I worked directly in production: fixing bugs, monitoring the system, resolving database issues, and building on-demand features and reports.

## Now

These days I’m strengthening my system design skills and studying AI engineering. I write notes here about what I’m learning, and I’m open to working with international teams.
